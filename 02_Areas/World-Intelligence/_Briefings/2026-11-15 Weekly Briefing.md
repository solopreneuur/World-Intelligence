## Top Signals

1. **Nvidia's governance crisis finished its arc from personal scandal to legislative catalyst in a single week.** Jensen Huang was ousted (CFO Colette Kress named interim CEO, ex-Synopsys chief Harvey Jones the company's first-ever independent board chair), with Nvidia's market value falling below $4 trillion. A second, unrelated affair allegation — this one with a direct subordinate — collapsed the "espionage victim" framing his defenders had been building. Senate Banking Chair Tim Scott set a December 3 hearing and pulled Commerce Secretary Lutnick into it alongside Huang, converting a personal-conduct story into a formal inquiry on export-control vetting. By week's end, WSJ's "backdoor route" reporting became the most damaging document yet: Huang personally cultivated Southeast Asian data-center partners — including MegaSpeed, which allegedly arranged ~$12 billion in Nvidia chip shipments to China — while lobbying Washington to keep the overseas-data-center loophole open; the US and Singapore are now investigating MegaSpeed directly. Reuters separately documented 26 procurement records showing a defense-linked, blacklisted Chinese university buying H200 chips through resellers and cloud-rental schemes. None of this stopped a 100,000-signature employee petition to reinstate Huang from gathering rank-and-file and industry-elite support (Musk, Epic's Tim Sweeney, Y Combinator's Garry Tan) — support that may be boxing the board into holding the line, since reinstating him now would look like capitulation. *([WSJ](https://www.wsj.com/tech/jensen-huang-backdoor-ai-data-centers-a5e4499d?mod=rss_worldnews), [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/13/nvidias-huang-lutnick-summoned-to-testify-to-us-senate?traffic_source=rss), [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/11/chinese-university-buys-nvidia-ai-h200-chips-despite-us-ban-records-show?traffic_source=rss), [The Information](https://www.theinformation.com/briefings/thousands-nvidia-staff-sign-petition-bring-back-huang))*

2. **Three US-centered diplomatic and military fronts strained simultaneously, days before a historic Shenzhen trilateral that excludes Ukraine by design.** The White House confirmed the first-ever three-way Trump-Xi-Putin sitdown, with nuclear arms and the Iran war on the agenda but pointedly not Ukraine — the administration is already managing expectations downward. In that gap, Zelensky disclosed the US had floated a Donbas-for-NATO-membership land swap, a trial balloon he conditioned on a referendum plus a full security-guarantee package rather than an agreed framework. Iran struck Bahrain's Riffa radar site a second time in three days and mined two more tankers in Hormuz, undercutting any read of last week's strike as a one-off; a parallel WSJ report showed the administration's own intelligence estimate (up to 2,500 Iranian missiles remaining) contradicts Trump's public claims of degraded capability. And ASEAN closed its Philippines chairmanship roughly 80% of the way to a South China Sea code of conduct — a near-miss likely to be framed as "substantial progress" rather than a missed deadline. *([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/14/trump-xi-and-putin-to-hold-first-three-way-summit-in-china?traffic_source=rss), [SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/news/world/europe/article/3374450/zelensky-says-us-floated-idea-ukraine-ceding-donbas-nato-membership), [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/15/iran-says-it-targets-us-radar-in-bahrain-what-do-we-know?traffic_source=rss), [WSJ](https://www.wsj.com/world/middle-east/u-s-figures-iran-still-has-up-to-2-500-missiles-countering-trump-assertions-4c6f4d67?mod=rss_worldnews))*

3. **The AI capital stack's stress became an explicit monetary-policy variable, not just a market-watchers' debate.** Anthropic's IPO range compressed again (from ~$2T in early October to $1.1–1.75T this week), with one analyst reframing the listing as a credit event rather than an equity story. CoreWeave's revenue doubled to $3.66B, but interest expense ($846M against $46.9B in GPU-collateralized debt) now exceeds operating income. SoftBank wrote off $3.7B in non-refundable turbine advance payments — 39% of its total committed advances — the same week Anthropic locked in a new $3.4B turbine bet of its own. And Fed Governor Bowman put a number on it: AI-related demand now accounts for roughly a quarter of above-target core PCE inflation, and she warned of a stock correction if AI returns disappoint — a sitting Fed governor treating the buildout as a rate-setting input, not a valuation sideshow. *([The Information](https://www.theinformation.com/briefings/coreweave-reports-revenue-surge-huge-increase-cash-burn), [The Information](https://www.theinformation.com/briefings/softbank-writes-3-7-billion-advance-payments-data-center-projects), [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-10/fed-s-bowman-warns-of-stock-correction-if-ai-bets-don-t-pan-out), [Net Interest](https://www.netinterest.co/p/a-bankers-guide-to-anthropic))*

4. **A synchronized global hawkish pivot moved from talk to data in a single print.** October CPI's hot reading flipped CME FedWatch odds on a December hike from 84% to 100% in a day; the 10-year Treasury broke above 5%; Dallas Fed's Logan said at least two more quarter-point hikes are needed, and Waller said he'd back a jumbo 50bp move if inflation stays hot. UK, US and eurozone policymakers gathered in London and all leaned hawkish, while PPI data showed wholesale-price broadening keeping separate pressure on the Fed. Australia's home loans posted a record quarterly drop after the RBA's own aggressive hikes — a preview of the credit crunch other central banks are walking toward with their eyes open. The one genuinely mixed signal: the NY Fed's consumer survey showed unemployment-expectation deterioration at its worst since April 2020 alongside job-finding expectations at their best since November 2025 — a labor market reshuffling, not cleanly tightening or loosening. *([WSJ](https://www.wsj.com/economy/cpi-inflation-report-october-2026-369f8032?mod=rss_socialeconomyfeed), [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-11/officials-from-major-central-banks-signal-more-rate-increases), [New York Fed](https://www.newyorkfed.org/newsevents/news/research/2026/20261109))*

5. **Two cascading, non-malicious critical-infrastructure failures hit the US in the same stretch of days — and a state actor's own infrastructure is proving no more resilient.** The FAA's nationwide ground stop, which disrupted nearly 10,000 flights, was traced to a routine router/copper-wire upgrade gone wrong; Microsoft's 74-country, 24-hour-plus outage days earlier was ruled out as a cyberattack too. Both share the same root cause — aging systems, human error — rather than anything more dramatic, which is exactly the unglamorous finding appropriators can build modernization funding around. Meanwhile Russia's statistics service still cannot fully publish core economic data two weeks after an actual breach, leaving Russian policymakers deciding on incomplete information during an already-stressed economy — state capacity for basic data infrastructure is fragile everywhere, attack or no attack. *([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/15/what-forced-the-us-to-ground-all-planes-a-router-switch-we-now-know?traffic_source=rss), [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/13/microsoft-hit-by-global-outages-what-happened-and-why?traffic_source=rss), [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-14/russia-struggles-to-restore-key-statistics-two-weeks-after-hack))*

6. **US-China decoupling kept compounding quietly on supply chains, ownership structures and talent at once.** Novelis, an Indian-owned US aluminum producer, sold off its wholly-owned Chinese subsidiaries for $265M; Nexperia's China unit's forced-decoupling fight escalated into administrators shopping a majority or minority stake to outside investors after Wingtech rejected handing over non-China operations; and a Harvard professor China poached is now actively recruiting other Chinese scholars to follow him home — the human-capital mirror image of Washington's hardware export controls, and a slower but potentially more durable competitive cost. Underneath it, Chinese labs including Tencent are reportedly starting to deliberately throttle the cybersecurity/hacking capability of their own frontier open-weight releases — a quiet admission that maximizing open-weight competitiveness against US closed models has real offensive-security costs, from the side with the most to gain from it. *([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-15/novelis-cuts-china-exposure-with-265-million-sale), [SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/tech/tech-war/article/3374320/court-appointed-administrators-sound-out-investors-nexperia-stake-amid-wingtech-feud), [WSJ](https://www.wsj.com/world/china/china-poached-this-top-harvard-professor-now-he-wants-others-to-join-him-1e03222d?mod=rss_worldnews))*

7. **AI-adjacent security and trust failures hit a different layer each day, now including a physical, combat-relevant one.** SCMP reported Iran used US troops' social-media accounts, fitness-app location data and ad-tracking identifiers to estimate base layouts before strikes — the same ad-tech-as-surveillance-infrastructure problem Citizen Lab flagged the prior week, now shown causing US casualties and billions in damage rather than just privacy exposure.
